# Ingredient catalog coverage review

Reviewed 2026-10-14. This compares the live shared `data/catalog.sqlite` against manufacturer ranges and MoreBeer's current homebrew listings. [Catalog source pages](catalog-sources.md) records the provider URLs for future reviews. Counts below describe **our database**, not the number of products offered by a provider. This is a prioritized discovery report, not an import manifest or a claim that every provider SKU was enumerated.

## Why Lighthouse is missing

The current catalog was built as a small, hand-curated sample. It has no recurring provider-range audit or ingestion process. It has 17 Briess grain records, 11 Weyermann, two Crisp, two Simpsons, and no BESTMALZ, Rahr, Viking, Great Western, or other maltster records. Briess's [current product range](https://brewingwithbriess.com/products/) lists **Lighthouse™ Munich Malt**, and its [technical sheet](https://brewingwithbriess.com/wp-content/uploads/documents/Briess-PISB-Lighthouse-Munich-Malt-040524.pdf) gives 6 SRM and other typical values. There is no corresponding catalog row. This is a coverage gap, not a problem with catalog search.

The catalog now also has 12 Briess CBW extract records in `Other fermentables`, but the broader ingredient range remains thin: eight hop products, eight yeast products, and seven nutrients. MoreBeer lists [more than 75 malt and grain types](https://morebeer.com/collections/brewing-malt-grain), [23 extract listings](https://www.morebeer.com/category/malt-extract.html), and [83 pellet-hop listings](https://morebeer.com/collections/pellet-hops). These retailer listing counts include packaging and merchant-specific products, so they are discovery signals rather than direct targets for our record counts.

## Catalog entry rules

1. Make one catalog product for a distinct manufacturer formulation or malt variant. Do not duplicate it for package sizes, whole versus crushed grain, retailer aliases, or lot-specific analyses. Lots can override crop-dependent values.
2. Use the manufacturer product page or technical sheet as the primary source for product name, yield, color, attenuation, and images. A retailer may identify a gap or provide a purchase link, but its range often mixes products, kits, packaging, and aliases.
3. Preserve units and measurement context from the source. For example, Lighthouse's 6 SRM is a grain color value. An extract's SRM needs its stated test concentration before it can be used as a comparable calculation input. Leave unknown values null.
4. Add products, specs, links, and copied images directly to the authoritative SQLite catalog. Preserve IDs and existing rows; apply repeatable upserts so a range can be revisited safely. Back up and integrity-check the database before and after a large addition. The [source registry](catalog-sources.md) tracks provider pages, not product rows.
5. Review source ranges periodically. New or renamed products should be matched by manufacturer and product identity before inserting, and discontinued products should remain resolvable for existing recipes.
